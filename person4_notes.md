## 1. Porter's Five Forces: UK streaming (team judgement)

All ratings below are **our team's judgement**, based on the evidence log in section 3. They are not measured scores.

| Force | Rating (judgement) | Why | What it means for Netflix Together |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buyer power** | **High** *(focus)* | Monthly plans and no contracts, so switching cost is close to zero. UK prices rose on 3 Sep 2026 to £7.99 / £13.99 / £20.99 (ads plan +33%), which raises price sensitivity. | Retention has to be earned each month. A feature that makes shared viewing easier gives households a reason to stay. |
| **Rivalry** | **High** *(focus)* | Disney+, Prime Video, Apple TV+, NOW and BBC iPlayer/ITVX all compete for the same evening. Rivals compete on content and price, and less on choosing *together*. | Differentiating on the choosing experience is harder for rivals to copy than a single title. |
| **Threat of substitutes** | **High** *(focus)* | YouTube, social video, gaming and free broadcast TV. When choosing takes too long, the evening goes to a substitute. | "Less scrolling. More watching." attacks the moment substitutes win. |
| **Supplier power** | **Medium** | Studios and talent hold strong IP, but Netflix's own productions reduce its reliance on them. | Little direct effect. The feature works with the existing catalogue. |
| **Threat of new entrants** | **Low–Medium** | Content budgets and scale are high barriers. Niche or FAST services can still enter. | Little direct effect. Scale in recommendation data is Netflix's advantage. |

**So what:** the three high forces (buyers, rivalry, substitutes) all concern keeping households engaged. Netflix Together targets the shared-evening decision, where all three meet.

## 2. Short VRIO: Netflix capabilities behind Netflix Together (team judgement)

| Resource / capability | Valuable? | Rare? | Hard to imitate? | Organised to exploit? | Implication (judgement) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recommender system + viewing data at scale | Yes | Yes (few rivals at this scale) | Partly (data and know-how build up over years) | Yes | Source of advantage. Underpins a 3-film shortlist for 2+ viewers. |
| AI Discover mood-based TV experience (US test) | Yes | Yes for now | No (UI ideas can be copied) | Yes (run as a "test kitchen") | A base to build on, not an advantage by itself |
| Same-TV group choice with private votes | Yes (hypothesis) | We found no evidence of a major streamer offering this | Partly (early-mover learning) | Not yet. Needs a pilot | Potential temporary advantage if tested and refined first |
| Financial capacity to test (see Q3 2026 figures) | Yes | No (big rivals also have budgets) | No | Yes | Parity. Makes a focused UK pilot affordable. |

We don't claim the feature would be "unique": we found no evidence of a comparable feature.

## 4. Speaker notes (about 2:00, ~280 words)

**[0:00–0:20] Claim.**
"Our claim on this slide: Netflix Together fits Netflix's strategy where rivals' group features don't. The ratings in this table are our team's judgement, based on the sources in the footer."

**[0:20–0:45] Netflix today and AI Discover.**
"Netflix today means one profile, a lot of scrolling, and nothing built for two people choosing together. On 3 November Netflix launched AI Discover, a US, English-language, TV-only test. You pick a mood such as 'scare me', which shortens the choice, but it is personalised to one profile. It doesn't help two people agree."

**[0:45–1:05] Watch-party tools.**
"Watch-party tools solve a different problem: syncing separate screens. Disney+ removed GroupWatch in 2023, and Hulu's Watch Party works in a web browser only. We found no evidence of a major streamer offering private voting for two or more people on one TV."

**[1:05–1:30] Fit and benefits.**
"Netflix Together extends AI Discover's mood idea: a 3-film shortlist, private votes and a mutual match, all on the same TV. The benefits are hypotheses for a UK pilot to test: faster choice, higher satisfaction and better retention. Retention matters more now that UK prices rose on 3 September to £7.99, £13.99 and £20.99."

**[1:30–1:50] Costs.**
"For costs, we name categories, not figures: design, engineering, recommender changes for two people, testing, and maintenance. Netflix can afford a focused test: Q3 revenue was $12.87 billion with a 34.6% operating margin. Ad-revenue guidance was cut to about $2.7 billion, so engagement matters. These numbers show capacity to invest, not the return."

**[1:50–2:00] Close.**
"Our positioning: *Less scrolling. More watching.* That is the one-line reason Netflix should test this in the UK."

Delivery tips: point at the red row when you say "Netflix Together". Say "hypotheses" clearly. Don't put a number on costs or benefits if asked; say the pilot is designed to measure them.
